The Big Idea

A mission can end. Its legacy doesn't.

Spacecraft are designed to explore places humans cannot easily reach. Some stop communicating, some lose power, and others reach the end of their operational lives.

But a silent rover is not a forgotten achievement. The data it collected, the images it sent home and the challenges it overcame continue to help scientists understand Mars.

This project tells the stories of three explorers and asks a simple question: What remains after a space mission ends?

01 / THE PIONEER

Sojourner: The first steps

In 1997, Sojourner became the first rover to operate on Mars. This small robotic explorer tested technologies and examined nearby rocks, helping pave the way for future rover missions.

Its journey was short, but it proved that a mobile robot could explore the Martian surface.

02 / THE SURVIVOR

Spirit: A long journey

Spirit landed on Mars in January 2004. It was designed for a mission lasting about 90 Martian days, but it worked for years, examining rocks, soil and the planet's ancient environment.

Spirit eventually became trapped in soft soil and could no longer move to a position where it could survive the Martian winter. NASA's final communication attempt was in 2011.

03 / THE MARATHON EXPLORER

Opportunity: Beyond expectations

Opportunity landed in 2004 and continued working for nearly 15 years. It travelled much farther than originally planned and found evidence that liquid water had once shaped parts of Mars.

A planet-wide dust storm in 2018 blocked sunlight from reaching its solar panels. After repeated attempts to re-establish contact, NASA declared the mission complete in February 2019.
